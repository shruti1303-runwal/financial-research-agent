# Greenfield Energy Ltd. — Annual Report FY2025

*Fictional sample company created for demonstration purposes. All figures in INR crore unless stated.*

## Chairman's Message

Greenfield Energy delivered steady results in FY2025 despite lower power tariffs in several states. Revenue increased to ₹8,940 crore from ₹8,210 crore in FY2024. Our installed renewable capacity reached 6.8 GW, up from 5.1 GW, as we commissioned new solar parks in Rajasthan and Gujarat and our first hybrid wind-solar project in Karnataka. Chairman Rajiv Menon noted that the company signed power purchase agreements (PPAs) for an additional 2.2 GW during the year.

## Financial Highlights

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 7,350 | 8,210 | 8,940 |
| EBITDA | 5,880 | 6,490 | 7,150 |
| Net profit | 610 | 702 | 760 |
| Earnings per share (₹) | 3.8 | 4.4 | 4.75 |
| Installed capacity (GW) | 4.0 | 5.1 | 6.8 |

The EBITDA margin was 80.0% in FY2025, reflecting the low operating costs of renewable generation. Net profit is lower relative to revenue because of high interest and depreciation costs.

## Capacity and Operations

Solar contributes 4.9 GW of installed capacity, wind 1.4 GW and hybrid projects 0.5 GW. The average plant load factor (capacity utilisation) for solar was 25.1% and for wind 31.8%. Electricity generation for the year was 13,600 million units (FY2024: 10,900 million units). The average realised tariff fell to ₹3.05 per unit from ₹3.20 per unit.

## Balance Sheet and Financing

Total debt stood at ₹38,500 crore at year-end and shareholders' equity at ₹9,600 crore, giving a debt-to-equity ratio of about 4.0. Finance costs were ₹3,450 crore. During the year we refinanced ₹6,000 crore of loans through green bonds at an average interest rate of 7.6%, lowering our average cost of debt from 9.1% to 8.6%. Capital expenditure was ₹11,200 crore.

## Key Risks

- **High leverage:** debt-to-equity of about 4x leaves the company sensitive to rising interest rates. A 1 percentage point rise in interest rates would increase annual finance costs by roughly ₹180 crore on floating-rate debt.
- **Tariff pressure:** competitive auctions continue to push tariffs down.
- **Counterparty risk:** delayed payments from some state electricity distribution companies (DISCOMs); receivables older than 90 days were ₹640 crore.
- **Weather and climate variability:** lower solar irradiation or wind speeds reduce generation.
- **Supply chain:** dependence on imported solar modules and policy changes on import duties.

## Outlook

Greenfield targets 10 GW of installed capacity by FY2027, including 1.5 GW of battery energy storage projects. Management guides FY2026 revenue growth of 15% to 18%, supported by capacity already under construction (2.4 GW).
